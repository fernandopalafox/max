# 2026-05-06

## Goal

We're trying to debug why fine-tuning with regularized LoRA-XS models is worse than expected. The geometry results from 2026-05-05 show that regularized pretraining produces near-perfect A geometry and better-conditioned FIM, so the adaptation should be easier — but empirically it isn't. Today's experiments tried to identify where the bottleneck is.

## LR Sweep (loraxs_regularized fine-tuning)

Checked whether the default lr=0.0003 is leaving adaptation performance on the table. Ran 4 fine-tuning jobs per seed (seeds 1 and 2) with LRs at 1x, 10x, 100x, 1000x the baseline:

| multiplier | lr     |
|------------|--------|
| 1x         | 0.0003 |
| 10x        | 0.003  |
| 100x       | 0.03   |
| 1000x      | 0.3    |

Config: `configs/adapt_loraxs_regularized_pt_sft.json` with `trainer.lr` overridden. W&B project `regularized_lr`, grouped by LR. Pretrained checkpoints: `baseline_loraxs_regularized/run_1` and `run_2`.

**Result:** Increasing LR did not change fine-tuning performance. All four LRs converged to similar reward, ruling out step size as the bottleneck.

## R Dynamics Experiment

Checked whether the geometry difference between regularized and vanilla loraxs checkpoints translates into meaningfully different R dynamics during OGD fine-tuning. Tracked per fine-tuning step:
- `r_displacement` = ‖R − R₀‖_F (cumulative displacement from init)
- `r_grad_norm` = ‖∇_R L‖_F (gradient norm w.r.t. R)

W&B project `r_dynamics`, grouped by `trainer_type`. Ran loraxs_regularized seeds 1–2 and loraxs seeds 1–2 (4 runs total, 50k steps each).

**Result:** Vanilla loraxs and regularized loraxs show roughly the same gradients and displacement. Better geometry at pretraining is not producing larger or more stable gradients during fine-tuning — the two conditions are mechanically indistinguishable from R's perspective.

## Cfix Regularized Pretraining (loraxs_reg_Cfix)

The existing `tdmpc2_regularized` trainer computes C_hat by concatenating activations from all H=3 timesteps of the horizon rollout, giving (H*B, rank) = (768, 48) samples per layer per step. Since each z_t is produced autoregressively from z_{t-1}, these samples are temporally correlated — the same latent trajectory thread runs through all three timesteps. This means C_hat is estimated from far fewer independent directions than 768, which could cause the L_B loss to push toward a distorted covariance target and corrupt the geometry we're trying to achieve.

The fix: compute C_hat using only the t=0 activations, i.e. `Bh = bh_t0[i]` with shape (B, rank) = (256, 48). These B samples come from different replay buffer entries (different initial observations and actions), so they are approximately i.i.d. and give an unbiased estimate of the true bottleneck covariance.

New trainer type: `tdmpc2_regularized_Cfix` in `max/trainers.py`. Config: `configs/pretrain_loraxs_reg_Cfix_tdmpc2.json`. Save dir: `data/models/cheetah/baseline_loraxs_reg_Cfix/`.

Launched 4 pretraining seeds (GPUs 0–3):

```bash
GPUS=(0 1 2 3)
RUNS=(1 2 3 4)
for i in 0 1 2 3; do
    conda run -n max python scripts/train.py \
        --config configs/pretrain_loraxs_reg_Cfix_tdmpc2.json \
        --save_dir data/models/cheetah/baseline_loraxs_reg_Cfix/run_${RUNS[$i]} \
        --run-name loraxs_reg_Cfix_r${RUNS[$i]} \
        --seed ${RUNS[$i]} \
        --gpu ${GPUS[$i]} &
done
wait
```

W&B run names: `loraxs_reg_Cfix_r1` through `loraxs_reg_Cfix_r4`.

## Geometry Evaluation (MPPI policy, training env)

Re-ran `eval_geometry.py` with the random-policy collection replaced by MPPI-guided rollouts, mirroring the training evaluator exactly (same planner config, training environment at mass_scale=1.0). Old random-policy results discarded. 4 seeds per trainer type, 1024 (z, a) samples per run.

| trainer_type         | A^T A diag | A^T A off-diag | C_hat diag | C_hat off-diag |
|----------------------|------------------:|-----------------:|------------------:|-----------------:|
| loraxs               | 14.3646 ± 0.1376  | 1.4739 ± 0.0326  | 1.0818 ± 0.1114   | 0.7056 ± 0.2061  |
| loraxs_regularized   |  1.0001 ± 0.0002  | 0.0008 ± 0.0000  | 0.9235 ± 0.0469   | 0.3839 ± 0.0355  |
| loraxs_reg_Cfix      |  0.9999 ± 0.0001  | 0.0006 ± 0.0001  | 0.8808 ± 0.0225   | 0.4301 ± 0.0183  |
| loraxs_init          |  1.0001 ± 0.0000  | 0.0000 ± 0.0000  | 76.5488 ± 1.4315  | 38.5338 ± 0.7213 |

Takeaways: A^T A geometry is unchanged from before (regularized variants nail orthonormality, vanilla loraxs explodes, loraxs_init is exact by construction). C_hat results shift meaningfully under a realistic state distribution vs random policy: loraxs now has diag ≈ 1 (near-identity on the diagonal) but very high off-diagonal correlation (0.71), meaning the activations are well-scaled but heavily correlated. loraxs_regularized achieves much lower off-diagonal (0.38) and near-unit diagonal (0.92), confirming the whitening loss is doing real work under the training distribution. loraxs_reg_Cfix is slightly worse than loraxs_regularized on both metrics, consistent with the random-policy eval. loraxs_init C_hat is enormous — the SVD initialization scales B columns by large singular values, making the activations wildly ill-conditioned.
